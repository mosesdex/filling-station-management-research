The two halves

Idea 1 and Idea 2, built as one product

The proposal put two options on the table. This prototype merges them, because they share a spine: a delivery in the station app is a completed trip in the supply platform, and a station’s days-of-stock is exactly the input the allocation engine needs.

Idea 1 · protects margin

Station accountability

  • Daily report in under 3 minutes, offline-tolerant
  • Meters, dips, delivery, takings split, expenses, photo evidence
  • Server computes litres, expected cash, shortage, tank variance
  • Per-tank variance trend alerts (the theft detector)
  • Attendant shortage league across all ten stations
  • Price broadcast with acknowledgement, compliance calendar, CNG module
Idea 2 · grows revenue

Bulk supply & haulage

  • Customer ordering portal with contract pricing and live truck tracking
  • Per-compartment chain of custody: seals at load, dips at discharge
  • Telematics integrated, not built: deviation, idling and fuel-level feeds
  • Trip P&L, credit limits and ageing, proof of delivery, invoicing
  • Allocation engine splitting each inbound load: retail vs bulk
  • Fleet card honoured at Weeldrop’s own ten stations, so no acceptance risk

The five-minute demo

Every number below is live in the prototype. Nothing is a static image.

1 · Manager submits, offline
Mobile → Station Manager → “Submit today’s figures”. Edit a closing meter and watch litres sold recompute. Step through dips, delivery, takings, photos.
2 · The queue drains
Submit with no signal. The report is saved on the phone, then uploads itself when the network returns.
3 · Owner opens the app at 9pm
Mobile → Owner. Nine stations green, Bodija red, Jembewon and Nihort amber.
4 · Drill into Bodija
Tank 2 variance has drifted for nine days against its own baseline. Open the dip photo. Order a surprise dip. Call the manager.
5 · Swipe to the fleet
WD-04 has been stationary 41 minutes, 3.2 km off the approved corridor, carrying 30,000 L of someone’s diesel.

What the demo proves

  • The manager types raw readings only; no derived figure is trusted from a device
  • A shortage and a variance trend are two different signals, caught two different ways
  • Offline is the normal case, not an error state
  • The owner gets exceptions, not a feed: nine normal stations stay silent
  • Retail and haulage sit in one app because they are one business
Fictional data, real structure. Station names, products and the Ibadan geography are the client’s. Volumes, cash, staff names, customers and trips are invented for the demo, but they reconcile: takings are derived from litres × price, so every figure ties out.
