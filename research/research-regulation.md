## 1. NMDPRA — the regulator for retail filling stations

### 1.1 Role and legal basis
- The **Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA)** was established by the **Petroleum Industry Act (PIA) 2021, s.29**, replacing the mid/downstream functions of the defunct Department of Petroleum Resources (DPR). It is both technical/safety regulator and commercial/market supervisor for everything from refining to retail. The upstream equivalent is NUPRC.
  - Source: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/oil-gas-electricity/1747608/... (Advocaat Law Practice article, Feb 2026) — *legal-professional, high quality*
  - Source: https://www.nmdpra.gov.ng/ — *official*
- The PIA adopts a **licence-centric model**: conducting any midstream/downstream operation without the relevant NMDPRA licence is prohibited (PIA s.125). Unauthorized dealing in PMS/AGO/DPK/LPG is a criminal offence.
  - Source: Mondaq article above — *legal-professional*
  - Source: https://www.tekedia.com/available-upstream-downstream-oil-gas-sector-licenses-in-nigeria-all-you-need-to-know/ — *blog/business-media; consistent with legal sources*
- **Section 31 PIA**: NMDPRA must establish an "efficient, safe and non-discriminatory" midstream/downstream market. **Section 33 PIA**: NMDPRA can fix and collect fees, and imposes a **0.5% levy on the wholesale price of petroleum products and natural gas sold in Nigeria**, payable into regulatory/Midstream-Downstream Gas Infrastructure funds. This levy forms part of the wholesale price chain that stations ultimately buy through.
  - Source: Mondaq article — *legal-professional*
  - Corroborated by Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Fees Regulations 2024 gazette, reg. 4: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig232456.pdf — *official gazette*
- NMDPRA operates under overall policy direction of the Minister of Petroleum Resources (PIA s.3); regulatory actions are operationally autonomous but policy-contingent — a real regulatory-risk factor cited by law firms.
  - Source: Mondaq article — *legal-professional*

### 1.2 Licensing path for a retail filling station
Licensing runs on NMDPRA's electronic portals: **CELPS (Central Electronic Licensing and Permit System)** and **ROMS (Retail Outlet Management System)** at celps.nmdpra.gov.ng.
- Source: https://celps.nmdpra.gov.ng/Account/Register — *official portal*

The classic staged process (inherited from DPR practice, still the operating pattern under NMDPRA) — from a business-media walkthrough, **[UNCERTAIN on exact current numeric standards — blog-quality source; verify against current NMDPRA guidelines before relying]**:
1. **Site Suitability Inspection / pre-approval** — application via lawyer to NMDPRA; site criteria historically include: not in pipeline or high-tension cable right-of-way; ≥15 m from road edge to nearest pump; max 4 stations within a 2 km stretch of road; ≥400 m from an existing station; drainage must not discharge to a stream/river; Federal Ministry of Transportation consent if on a federal highway; NMDPRA-supervised EIA by accredited consultant.
2. **Approval/Licence to Construct (ATC/LTC)** — requires certificate of incorporation, memart, tax clearance, police report, fire certificate, approved building plan, surveyor-general letter, deed of assignment; EIA report mandatory for underground storage >270,000 litres; development must start within 3 months or approval lapses.
3. **Licence to Operate / Storage & Sales Licence** — requires pressure-test/leak-detection certificates for underground tanks, station photographs, fire certificate, evidence of trained pump attendants, fee scaled to storage capacity. Physical requirements include perimeter wall (≥1.5 m), drainage, fire extinguishers and sand buckets on each pump island, "No Smoking"/"No cellphone" signage, canopy over PMS pumps, price billboard with changeable-price mechanism, standby generator, potable water.
4. **Takeover/transfer** procedures apply when a station changes hands (see transfer fees in 1.3).
5. Ongoing conduct rules: station manager always present, pumps must dispense accurately and display volume/price, trained attendants in safety gear, engines off during fueling, good housekeeping.
  - Source: Tekedia article above — *blog/business-media; detailed but not an official checklist*
- Retail outlet licences are typically **valid 1 year (some sources) / licence-to-operate renewal cycles of 1–2 years (official fee schedule implies "every two years... per annum" wording)**. **[CONFLICTING — see fee table: LTO listed "New/Renewal every two years, NGN 100,000 per annum"; social-media sources say 1-year renewable. Practical takeaway: renewal is a recurring annual/biennial cost.]**

## 2. PIA 2021 impact on downstream/retail

- PIA 2021 is the biggest petroleum-sector reform since the Petroleum Act 1969: it split regulation (NUPRC upstream / NMDPRA mid+downstream), commercialized NNPC into NNPC Ltd, and stipulated **market-based pricing principles for petroleum products** (ss.123(1), 205, 207 referenced in IMF analysis).
  - Source: https://pia.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/PIA-2021_compressed-1.pdf — *official*
  - Source: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2022/034/article-A001-en.xml (IMF Selected Issues Paper) — *official/IGO*
  - Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10468656/ — *peer-reviewed academic*
- Despite the PIA's pricing provisions, the government kept PMS at a regulated price after 2021, awaiting a "feasible framework" agreed with labour — full deregulation only came with the June 2023 subsidy removal (see §3).
  - Source: IMF paper above — *official/IGO*
- For retail operators the PIA means: (a) every activity needs its own licence (layered compliance, concurrent obligations, suspension/revocation risk); (b) NMDPRA regulates product quality, facility safety, environmental compliance (environmental management plans, remediation, decommissioning obligations — PIA s.102); (c) market-conduct oversight and price administration powers remain with NMDPRA even in a "deregulated" market (PIA s.110), so political price intervention is a live risk; (d) 0.5% wholesale levy embedded in cost chain.
  - Source: Mondaq/Advocaat article — *legal-professional*
- Sanctions ladder: administrative fines, licence suspension/revocation, plus civil/criminal liability for fuel-quality or safety failures.
  - Source: Mondaq/Advocaat article — *legal-professional*

## 3. Subsidy removal (2023) and price deregulation

### 3.1 Timeline and magnitude
- **29 May 2023**: President Tinubu's inaugural declaration "subsidy is gone". Pump price roughly tripled from ~NGN 185–195/L to ~NGN 500–540/L within days.
  - Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/nigeria-petrol-prices-soar-record-high-after-subsidy-removal-2023-07-18/ — *news (wire, high quality)*
  - Source (academic corroboration of 185.64% first-phase rise from N195): https://unjpe.com/index.php/UNJPE/article/download/318/276/528 — *academic*
- **18 July 2023**: NNPC raised prices again to **NGN 617/L** (from 557), then the highest ever. Subsidy had cost the government ~**USD 10bn in 2022** (Reuters) / ~**NGN 4trn per year** (Mordor Intelligence estimate).
  - Source: Reuters above — *news*; https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/nigeria-petrol-station-market — *market research*
- After subsidy removal, **56 private firms were licensed to import petrol**, ending NNPC's import monopoly (NNPC had been sole importer via crude-swap contracts).
  - Source: Reuters above, quoting NMDPRA CEO Farouk Ahmed — *news citing official*
- Prices continued climbing with naira depreciation (NGN ~460/USD early 2023 → beyond NGN 1,700/USD late 2025 per Mordor; ~N1,367/USD average July 2026 per MEMAN data). By late 2025–mid 2026 pump prices moved in the **NGN 865–1,250/L** band depending on period and location.
  - Sources: Mordor report — *market research*; https://discoveryalert.com.au/dangote-petrol-vs-imported-fuel-nigeria-pricing-2026/ — *analyst blog citing MEMAN data*; https://guardian.ng/news/petrol-price-may-hit-n1400-litre-as-dangote-refinery-loading-stalls/ — *news*
- Deregulation is not irreversible in practice: Mordor notes 2025 episodes of tariff hikes and **state-level price caps that squeezed retailer margins below breakeven for ~six weeks**, plus an **October 2025 15% refined-product import tariff** — evidence of continuing policy volatility. **[UNCERTAIN — single market-research source for the six-week caps episode and the 15% tariff; corroborate before quoting as fact.]**
  - Source: Mordor report — *market research*

### 3.2 Dangote refinery effect on the supply chain
- **Dangote Petroleum Refinery** (Lekki, Lagos; nameplate 650,000 bpd; among world's largest single-train refineries) started diesel/jet output January 2024 and **petrol (PMS) in September 2024**.
  - Source: https://www.dataphyte.com/issue/marina-maitama/2025/09/nupeng-vs-dangote-refinery-a-brewing-clash-over-union-rights-and-fuel-distribution — *data-journalism/news*
- Pricing structure: two channels — **coastal price** (marine delivery to coastal depots) and **gantry/ex-depot price** (truck loading at the refinery). E.g., March 2026: coastal N948/L vs gantry N995/L; July 2026: coastal N1,195/L vs gantry N1,215/L.
  - Sources: Guardian article above — *news*; discoveryalert analysis — *analyst blog citing MEMAN*
- Dangote has repeatedly cut and raised ex-depot prices with crude/naira movements: e.g., cut to **N825/L (27 Feb 2025)** with partner stations retailing N860–895; **N820/L (Sept 2025)** with direct free delivery to registered stations starting in 11 states; **N739/L (Dec 2025)** direct retail entry, undercutting import parity ~8%; back up to ~N1,215/L gantry by late July 2026 as Brent hit ~$90.
  - Sources: https://refinery.dangote.com/category/press-release/ — *official (company)*; Dataphyte — *news*; Mordor — *market research*; discoveryalert — *analyst blog*
- **Local vs import competitiveness flips repeatedly**: between Oct 2025 and Jul 2026 the cheaper source (Dangote gantry vs import landed cost) reversed at least four times; late July 2026 Dangote was cheaper by only ~N8/L (N1,215 vs import parity N1,223.32).
  - Source: discoveryalert citing MEMAN Energy Bulletin — *analyst blog on official association data*
- **Concentration risk**: when Dangote suspended gantry loading (e.g., March 2026 amid a crude price spike), depots repriced to N1,200–1,230/L and marketers warned of N1,400/L; PETROAN members could not load; NARTO said only select marketers (MRS, NNPC Retail) were lifting. Marketers explicitly warn a single dominant supply source exposes the market to volatility. Dangote receives ~5 crude cargoes/month from NNPC vs ~13 needed, forcing dollar-priced crude imports — importing FX volatility into pump prices.
  - Source: Guardian (Mar 2026) — *news*; https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/energy/1720461-new-petrol-price-depots-announce-rates-dangote-suspends-gantry-loading/ — *news (popular)*
- Distribution shift: Dangote deployed CNG truck fleets and **free direct delivery to registered stations** (also buying 6,000 dry-cargo trucks), disintermediating depots/tanker middlemen — a key driver of the Sept 2025 NUPENG dispute (see §5). Gasoline import cargoes fell to a 9-year low by Feb 2026 (~50,000 bpd), with NMDPRA reporting Dangote receipts (40m litres in Jan 2026) overtaking imports for the first time.
  - Sources: Dataphyte — *news*; Guardian citing Kpler and NMDPRA — *news*
- IPMAN signed a direct purchase agreement with Dangote Refinery (bulk-purchase arrangement for independents).
  - Source: https://www.cnbcafrica.com/companies/independent-petroleum-marketers-association-of-nigeria — *news* **[UNCERTAIN on current terms — headline-level evidence only]**

## 4. Compliance requirements for operating stations

- **Licence set** (see §1.2/1.3): LTE → LTC → LTO (renewable), plus modification/rebranding/tank authorisations as applicable, all via NMDPRA CELPS/ROMS portals. — *official gazette + portal*
- **Safety/technical standards**: now consolidated under the Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Operations Regulations 2025 (technical, safety, operational standards) per Advocaat. Physical station requirements historically enforced: fire extinguishers + sand buckets per pump island, signage, canopy, wall fence, drainage that avoids watercourses, trained attendants, manager on site, alternative power. — *legal-professional + blog (see §1.2 caveat)*
- **Tank integrity**: pressure tests / leak-detection tests with certificates at licensing and takeover; tank calibration authorisation NGN 10,000/tank (official fee schedule). — *official gazette*
- **Environmental**: mandatory Environmental Site Assessment, Environmental Management Plans, post-impact assessments, environmental audits with prescribed fees (Fourth Schedule, Fees Regs 2024); EIA required at siting stage; PIA s.102 imposes environmental management, remediation and decommissioning obligations. — *official gazette + legal-professional*
- **Pump accuracy (Weights & Measures)**: separate from NMDPRA — the **Weights and Measures Department of the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment** (legal metrology) inspects dispensing pumps; documented enforcement includes sealing three pumps at NNPC Mega Station Abuja for under-dispensing (~0.35 L short per 20 L) and sealing an over-registering LPG scale at an Oando station. Verification/inspection is a statutory, recurring exposure for stations.
  - Source: https://fmino.gov.ng/dispensing-nnpc-mega-filling-station-pumps-sealed-weights-measures-department/ — *official (FG ministry site; 2018 case — old but demonstrates the standing mechanism)* **[UNCERTAIN — current inspection frequency/fees for dispensers not confirmed from an official current source; NMDPRA also runs measurement rules via Petroleum Measurement Regulations 2023.]*
- **NMDPRA measurement regime**: Petroleum Measurement Regulations 2023 exist (fees folded into 2024 Fees Regs, Third Schedule "measurement systems"). — *legal-professional (Aluko & Oyebode) + official gazette*
- **Product pricing conduct**: stations must sell at prevailing market/approved prices and display prices; NMDPRA retains market-conduct and price-administration powers (PIA s.110). — *legal-professional*
- **Other agencies stations interact with**: SON (standards), NOSDRA (spills), FRSC (tanker/road safety), state fire services, plus federal/state tax. MEMAN lists NMDPRA, NOSDRA, SON, FRSC as its regulatory counterparts. — Source: https://moman.org/about-meman/ — *official (association)*

## 5. Industry bodies

- **MEMAN — Major Energies Marketers Association of Nigeria** (renamed from MOMAN on 31 Jan 2024): the "majors" — 11 Plc (ex-Mobil), Ardova, Conoil, MRS Oil, NNPC Retail, TotalEnergies Marketing Nigeria. Roughly a third of the petrol market (Reuters, 2023). Runs jetties (Apapa), publishes an Energy Bulletin with import-parity and ex-depot price data widely cited in the press; committees on HSSEQ, engineering/metrology, legal/policy.
  - Sources: https://moman.org/about-meman/ — *official (association)*; Reuters — *news*
- **IPMAN — Independent Petroleum Marketers Association of Nigeria**: the mass membership body of independent marketers who historically load from NNPC/private depots; independents operate **roughly 60% of Nigeria's ~31,220 stations** (Mordor). National spokesman frequently quoted: Chinedu Ukadike. Post-Dangote, IPMAN negotiated direct-purchase deals with the refinery and advocates restricting imports when local supply suffices (FX-conservation argument).
  - Sources: https://ipmanibadandepot.com/ — *association (chapter site)*; Mordor — *market research*; discoveryalert — *analyst blog*
- **PETROAN — Petroleum Products Retail Outlets Owners Association of Nigeria**: represents retail outlet owners specifically; National President Dr Billy Gillis-Harry. Post-subsidy-removal it sought a **N100bn support grant** for ~10,000 marketers; supports deregulation but lobbies for price-stability rules (e.g., proposed 6-month price-change windows) and activation of state refineries; its members were locked out during the March 2026 Dangote loading halt.
  - Sources: https://www.facebook.com/nmdpra.gov.ng/posts/... (NMDPRA page summary) — *official social*; https://guardian.ng/news/petroan-seeks-activation-of-state-refineries/ — *news*; Guardian Mar 2026 — *news*
- **NUPENG — Nigeria Union of Petroleum and Natural Gas Workers**: junior/mid-level oil & gas workers' union incl. tanker drivers, depot staff, station attendants; its strikes shut stations nationwide. September 2025: 2-day nationwide strike vs Dangote over anti-union driver contracts; stations shut in many states, black-market petrol hit N1,500/L in Calabar; resolved via DSS/Ministry-of-Labour-brokered MoU on unionisation. PENGASSAN is the senior-staff counterpart; NARTO represents transport owners.
  - Source: https://www.dataphyte.com/issue/marina-maitama/2025/09/nupeng-vs-dangote-refinery-a-brewing-clash-over-union-rights-and-fuel-distribution — *data-journalism*; https://www.channelstv.com/2025/09/07/nupeng-insists-on-strike-tells-nigerians-to-ignore-truck-drivers-association/ — *news*
- **How independents operate**: buy at ex-depot/gantry prices (from Dangote gantry, coastal depots, or private depot owners), truck product to their stations (or receive Dangote free delivery if registered), set pump prices competitively within local market bands; exposed to depot price swings, FX, and logistics costs; inland stations pay N50–80/L more than Lagos coastal stations (Mordor).
  - Sources: Guardian, Mordor, discoveryalert — *news/market research*

## 6. Station economics in the deregulated era

**Market size and structure** (Source: Mordor Intelligence, Jan 2026 update: https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/nigeria-petrol-station-market — *market research; proprietary estimates, treat point figures as indicative*):
- Nigeria petrol station market: **USD 20.88bn (2025) → USD 21.87bn (2026) → USD 28.17bn (2031F), 5.19% CAGR**.
- **~31,220 retail stations**: 12,950 north, 11,193 west, 7,077 east. Lagos ~3,000 sites.
- Throughput: Lagos **40,000–50,000 L/month per station** (saturation) vs **80,000–100,000 L/month** in Abuja/Port Harcourt.
- Fuel-only formats = 50.5% of 2025 revenue; gasoline = 65.1% of fuel revenue; LPG/CNG dispensing growing at 23.8% CAGR; NNPC targeting 100+ CNG outlets by 2026.
- Competitive landscape: NNPC Retail 1,000+ stations (after buying 380 Oando outlets, Dec 2024); TotalEnergies 577 stations (NGN 1.04trn 2024 revenue); Rainoil ~200 stations + 3 depots; Ardova/Conoil/MRS/11 Plc each 100–300 sites; independents ~60% of network.

**Margins** (multiple sources, varying quality):
- **Fuel gross margins ~8–12%**; **non-fuel (c-store/QSR) gross margins ~20–25%**; highway service plazas can earn ~30% higher margins than standalone pumps; food service up to 20% of site revenue at top urban TotalEnergies sites. (Mordor — *market research*) **[UNCERTAIN — percentage margins are Mordor estimates; per-litre naira margins fluctuate with each repricing.]**
- Observable per-litre spread (March 2026, Lagos): MEMAN ex-depot **N940–1,000/L** vs pump prices **N1,015–1,057/L** → indicative gross retail spread of roughly **N30–80/L** before logistics, levies and station costs. (Guardian market checks — *news*) 
- Dangote partner-station example (Feb 2025): ex-depot N825 → retail N860–895 → ~N35–70/L spread. (Dangote press release / discoveryalert — *official company + analyst*)
- Even N10–20/L pump-price differences shift customer traffic between stations (commercial drivers shop around). (Guardian — *news*)
- **Cost structure pressures**: deregulated procurement means working capital tied to ex-depot price × truckload volumes; retailers now carry **7–10 days of inventory vs 3–5 days global norm** because of supply uncertainty (Mordor); inland trucking adds N50–80/L; diesel for standby generators (diesel N1,428/L in Mar 2026 spike vs N929 in late Feb 2026 — Guardian); labour (attendant-service model dominant; self-service emerging in cities to cut labour cost — Mordor); recurring licence renewals, environmental filings, calibration, fire certificates (§1.3, §4).
- **Volatility**: pump prices flipped multiple times 2025–2026 (N739 direct-retail lows to N1,275 gantry highs); price-cap episodes pushed margins below breakeven temporarily (Mordor **[UNCERTAIN]**); a marketer buying a 45,000 L truckload at the wrong moment can be underwater on the whole cargo when prices fall ("small pricing errors could quickly turn cargoes unprofitable" — market commentary, Instagram/Legit — *low-quality source, but directionally consistent with news reporting*).
- **Diversification response**: operators add LPG/CNG, convenience retail, QSR, digital payments (43% of transactions via Flutterwave/Paystack per Mordor), and solar to defend margins in the post-subsidy era. (Mordor — *market research*)

## 7. Key uncertainty summary

1. **Site-standard specifics** (400 m spacing, 15 m setback, 4-stations-per-2 km) come from a blog restating legacy DPR-era rules; the governing text is now the Midstream & Downstream Petroleum Operations Regulations 2025, which was not directly reviewed. Verify before compliance use.
2. **Licence validity period** conflicting (1-year vs 2-year LTO cycle wording in the official fee schedule).
3. **Mordor figures** (station counts, throughput, margins, market size, Oct-2025 15% tariff, price-cap episode) are proprietary estimates from a single market-research source; cross-check any figure that becomes load-bearing.
4. **Pricing data is extremely volatile** — every naira figure here is a snapshot; the ex-depot/pump spread and Dangote-vs-import advantage flip within weeks.
5. **W&M pump-calibration enforcement** evidenced by an official but dated (2018) case; current inspection cadence/fees unconfirmed.
6. Some social-media-hosted sources (Facebook/Instagram/Legit posts) were used only for corroboration of price events, never as sole support for a structural claim.
