00 · Executive Summary

The opportunity in one page

Nigeria's downstream fuel market changed completely between 2023 and 2026. Subsidy removal (May 2023) and the Dangote refinery (petrol from September 2024) turned station ownership from a fixed-margin licence business into a volatile trading business. An owner's biggest risks are now (1) not knowing his real daily numbers fast enough, and (2) internal leakage — attendant tricks, cash skimming, short deliveries, and manager-level diversion.

The industry already has the ritual

Every serious station reconciles two physical measurements daily: pump totalizer (meter) readings and tank dip readings, checked against cash + POS + transfer takings. The math is standard and fully computable. Today it lives on paper — which is exactly why fraud survives.

Automation is how owners scale

Nigerian evidence: one operator grew from 18 to 89 stations after automating; another reclaimed leased-out stations and grew 73 → 176. Remote visibility is what lets an owner manage more stations than he can personally stand in.

The market gap is real

The competitive landscape is barbell-shaped: hardware-heavy enterprise systems (Epump, Smartflow, RockEye) on one end; generic single-phone ledger apps on the other. The middle — software-only, offline-first, daily-figures reporting with an owner dashboard — is nearly empty.

"Epump-grade visibility without the hardware." Managers submit meter readings, dip readings, and the cash/POS/transfer count in under 3 minutes — even offline. The owner opens his phone at 9pm and sees every station's litres sold, expected vs actual cash, and tank variance, with alerts when anything drifts.

Ten key findings

  1. Daily reconciliation is the product. The core loop — per-attendant meter reconciliation, per-tank dip-vs-book variance, payment split — is documented Nigerian audit practice. The app digitizes an existing ritual; it does not have to teach a new one.
  2. Variance trend analysis catches theft and leaks. Track each tank's cumulative % variance against its own "normal"; alert on trend breaks, not single-day noise.
  3. Fraud is well-documented and specific. A sourced inventory of attendant tricks, manager-level diversion (EFCC/NSCDC cases), and short deliveries — each maps to a concrete app control.
  4. Delivery verification is a first-class workflow — waybill vs per-compartment discharge vs before/after dips. This exact control would have caught a documented 26,700-litre diversion.
  5. Under-dispensing is regulatory survival. NMDPRA seals stations outright — 11 sealed in Rivers State in one day (Feb 2026).
  6. Offline-first is non-negotiable. 238 telco outages in January 2026; POS terminals halt when towers drop. Submissions must queue locally and sync later.
  7. Payments are a three-way split. Cash 33.6% / card 27.3% / transfer 13.6% at stations; 91% of stations run POS. The daily form captures the split natively.
  8. Data integrity beats data entry. Append-only records, camera-only photo evidence, GPS + server timestamps, and server-side computation of every derived number.
  9. No Nigerian competitor publishes pricing, and none is software-only. Transparent per-station Naira pricing with a free tier would be unique.
  10. Recommended stack: Expo/React Native + Supabase — row-level security for roles, pg_cron digests, local SQLite write-queue for offline.
01 · Industry Context

Nigeria's downstream fuel market, 2023–2026

The regulatory and market environment that shapes what a station owner needs from software.

The regulator: NMDPRA

The Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (created by the Petroleum Industry Act 2021, s.29) is the sole licensing, safety, and market-conduct regulator for retail stations. Operating without a licence is a criminal offence (PIA s.125). Licensing runs on the CELPS/ROMS e-portals through a staged chain: Licence to Establish → Licence to Construct → Licence to Operate (renewable), with siting, EIA, tank-integrity, and physical-standard requirements at each stage.

App implication: a per-station compliance calendar (licence expiry, calibration dates, fire certificate, environmental filings) is cheap to build and directly protects revenue — a lapsed licence or failed inspection means a sealed station.

Deregulation shock

  • 29 May 2023 — "subsidy is gone." Pump price roughly tripled within weeks (₦185–195 → ₦617/L by July 2023). The subsidy had cost ~$10bn in 2022.
  • 56 private import licences ended NNPC's import monopoly.
  • With naira depreciation, pump prices moved in a ₦865–1,250/L band through 2025–26.
  • Deregulation is not politically irreversible: 2025 saw state-level price-cap episodes that pushed retail margins below breakeven for weeks. [single-source]

The Dangote factor

  • The 650,000 bpd refinery (petrol from Sept 2024) now dominates supply, with two price channels — coastal (marine to depots) vs gantry (truck loading at the refinery). The Dangote-vs-import cost advantage flipped at least four times in nine months.
  • Free direct delivery to registered stations is disintermediating depots — and triggered the Sept 2025 NUPENG strike that shut stations nationwide.
  • Concentration risk: when Dangote suspended gantry loading (Mar 2026), depot prices spiked toward ₦1,400/L and many marketers simply could not load.

App implication: cost price changes per truckload, not per quarter. The app must record each delivery's actual unit cost and broadcast pump-price changes to all stations instantly — margin per litre is a daily-moving number worth first-class dashboard treatment.

Station economics

Margins

Fuel gross margin ~8–12%; observable ex-depot-to-pump spreads roughly ₦30–80/L. Non-fuel retail (shop, lubricants, LPG) earns 20–25%. Even ₦10–20/L price differences move traffic between stations.

Throughput

Lagos 40–50k L/month per station (saturated market) vs 80–100k L/month in Abuja / Port Harcourt. [market-research estimates]

Working capital

Depots demand payment upfront; retailers now hold 7–10 days of inventory (vs 3–5 global norm). A truckload bought at the wrong moment can be unprofitable end-to-end.

The four numbers that are the business — per station, per day: litres sold by product · gross margin per litre (pump price − delivered unit cost) · cash position · days of stock remaining. That is the owner dashboard.

02 · Station Operations

How a Nigerian filling station runs day to day

The operational playbook the app digitizes — assembled from Nigerian job ads, practitioner audit checklists, vendor training curricula, and a national newspaper investigation.

The two daily controls

1 · Tank dip

A calibrated dip stick lowered into each underground tank; the reading converts to litres via the tank's calibration chart. Larger operators use Automatic Tank Gauges (one Lagos vendor claims 1,000+ probes installed in Nigeria); independents mostly dip manually.

2 · Pump totalizer

Each nozzle's cumulative meter counter — not resettable in normal operation, which is what makes it auditable. Read at open, shift change, and close.

Shift reconciliation — the core math

litres_sold = closing_meter − opening_meter · per nozzle, per attendant
expected_takings = litres_sold × pump_price
shortage = (cash + POS + transfer + credit) − expected_takings

The cashier aggregates attendants; the manager reconciles the day. Standard closing checklist: sales summary → cash reconciliation → digital payments → credit review → inventory reconciliation → expenses → shift report before handover. Common failure points — counting errors, delayed entries, wrong nozzle readings — are all paper-process problems the app eliminates by computing everything server-side.

Wet stock management — where leaks and theft show up

book_stock = opening_stock + deliveries − metered_sales · per tank, per day
daily_variance = closing_dip − book_stock
cum_%_variance = cumulative_variance ÷ cumulative_sales × 100

Critical insight: every tank has a "normal" small persistent loss (temperature — petrol volume changes ~0.11% per °C — evaporation, meter calibration drift, delivery measurement error). The control objective is detecting a change from that normal trend, not chasing zero variance.

Investigation triggers (industry wetstock guidance): variance worsens by +0.1% vs the tank's monthly normal · an extra 10 L/day over a month · an extra 300 L/month. Nigerian regulatory tolerance figures were not found in sources — the trend method stands on its own. [global standard, flagged]

Deliveries and restocking

Supply chain today: pay the depot upfront → truck loads at Dangote gantry or coastal/private depot → tanker arrives with waybill. Verification procedure:

  1. Check waybill and meter tickets against the order before offloading
  2. Check compartment seals; dip/ullage each compartment against the waybill
  3. Dip the receiving tank before discharge
  4. Discharge one compartment at a time
  5. Dip again after settling (~5 min); delivered volume = after − before
  6. Water-paste check; note fuel temperature (warm fuel shrinks)
  7. Sign waybill; record delivery + unit cost in the stock book

03 · Fraud & Controls

The threat model the app defends against

Nigerian evidence throughout. Forum anecdotes are used for threat modelling (what happens), never for prevalence statistics (how often).

Customer-side fraud still hurts the owner: NMDPRA seals stations for under-dispensing, and reputational damage moves traffic to competitors in a market where customers actively watch the meter.

Manager-level fraud — the expensive tier

Sales diversion — real case

EFCC arrested the manager of an Emadeb Energy station over alleged diversion of ~₦500 million of his employer's sales (Oct 2025). Allegation stage, not conviction.

Diverted delivery — real case

A Nasarawa station manager received a 40,000 L truck but discharged only 13,300 L, diverting 26,700 L to sell at scarcity prices elsewhere (NSCDC arrest, reported by Premium Times / Vanguard).

Collusion & falsified records

Nigerian auditor checklists explicitly test for altered meter readings and attendant–manager collusion to under-report sales. Falsified dips are an inferred vector — checklists demand independent dip verification for a reason.

Detection: variance analysis at three levels

  • Shift level: per-attendant shortage = takings − (litres × price). Catches skimming same-day.
  • Tank level: daily variance = dip − book stock. Catches unrecorded sales, delivery shortfalls, leaks.
  • Trend level: cumulative % variance per tank vs its own baseline. Separates theft/leak (persistent one-direction drift) from benign noise (temperature, meter drift). Continuous reconciliation can isolate variance to a specific dispenser.

Regulatory survival: NMDPRA's "Operation One Litre for One Litre" seals stations outright for under-dispensing — 11 stations sealed in Rivers State in one day (Feb 2026). "It is either you're dispensing within the bandwidth or we shut you down." A sealed station is a 100% revenue stop; calibration tracking in the app is insurance, not bureaucracy.

The controls stack the app implements

  1. Per-shift, per-nozzle reconciliation — the atomic unit; catches skimming daily
  2. Daily tank variance + cumulative trend with per-tank baselines — alert on trend breaks, not noise
  3. Delivery verification workflow — ordered vs waybill vs per-compartment vs before/after dips, with photos. This is the control that would have caught the 26,700 L diversion.
  4. Dual/independent dips — recorded by one person, verified by another; flag divergence
  5. Separation of duties — who dispenses ≠ who counts ≠ who dips ≠ who reports
  6. Immutable records — no edits, only superseding corrections with full who/what/when history
  7. Cashless bias with offline tolerance — POS/transfer reduces skimmable cash; design for network failure
  8. Compliance dashboard — calibration due dates, licence renewals, seal status
  9. Surprise audits — owner triggers an unscheduled mid-day dip + meters + photos from his phone

Honesty note: the widely-quoted "30% of fuel budget lost to fraud" figure is uncited marketing — never use it in client materials. The sourced cases above are stronger evidence anyway.

05 · App Blueprint

Features, data model, architecture

Every design choice traces to evidence in the sections above.

The daily loop (MVP)

Manager submits (<3 min, offline-capable)

  • Per-nozzle opening/closing totalizer readings
  • Per-tank opening/closing dips
  • Deliveries: waybill no., volume, unit cost, before/after dips
  • Takings split: cash / POS / transfer / credit
  • Expenses with categories
  • Required photos: meter displays, dip stick, waybill

Server computes (never typed)

  • Litres sold per nozzle/product
  • Expected takings and per-shift shortage/overage
  • Tank variance vs book stock + cumulative trend
  • Margin per litre vs actual delivered cost
  • Days-of-stock estimates

Owner sees

  • All-stations overview: litres, revenue, cash, variance flags
  • Submission status — who hasn't reported
  • Push alerts: shortages, variance trend breaks, low stock, missing submissions
  • Nightly digest: one notification, all stations, exceptions first
  • Price broadcast with manager acknowledgement

Alert rules (initial, all configurable)

RuleTrigger
Missing submissionNot in by 21:30 WAT → nag manager · 22:30 → alert owner
Cash shortageShortage > X% of expected or > ₦Y absolute
Tank varianceDaily > 0.5% throughput → note · trend break vs baseline → alert
Delivery mismatchMeasured vs waybill gap > Z litres → immediate alert
Low stockDays-of-stock < N days
AnomalySubmission GPS far from station · device vs server clock mismatch

Data integrity layer — what makes manual figures trustworthy

  1. Append-only: managers get INSERT-only access; corrections are new rows referencing the original — the owner always sees who changed what, when
  2. Photo evidence: in-app camera only (no gallery uploads) for meters, dip stick, waybills
  3. Server time authoritative; device time recorded but never trusted
  4. GPS at capture; flag submissions far from the station's coordinates
  5. Server-side math: the manager never types litres-sold, expected cash, or variance
  6. Cross-checks: today's opening meter must equal yesterday's closing per nozzle — breaks flag missed or faked readings

Architecture

Stack

Expo / React Native + Supabase. JS-skilled teams onboard fastest on RN; Expo gives over-the-air updates (users won't update store apps); Supabase provides Postgres + row-level security + pg_cron + Edge Functions — one coherent story for relational, role-gated, report-heavy data. Flutter + Firebase is a legitimate alternative for a Dart-first team.

Offline-first (non-negotiable)

Local SQLite as on-device source of truth · write-ahead submission queue with idempotency keys · background drain with exponential backoff · visible pending / syncing / synced state per submission. Conflict story is deliberately trivial: one manager per station per day, server uniqueness on (station, date).

Roles & multi-tenancy

organizations → stations → memberships (role). Row-level security on every table; managers INSERT-only on their own station; owner reads everything in the org; service role runs the nightly digest crons. Multi-tenant from day one — the client's stations become tenant #1 if this becomes a product.

06 · Risks & Open Questions

What could make this fail — honestly stated

Contrarian views first, then the questions to settle with the client before writing code.

Garbage in, garbage out

The app's value rests on figures typed by the people it monitors. A colluding manager can fabricate a self-consistent day. Photos, GPS, meter-continuity checks, and variance trends raise the cost of lying substantially — a fake day must stay consistent with yesterday's photos and the tank's physical trend — but only hardware removes trust entirely. Position honestly: the app makes theft hard and visible, not impossible.

Adoption failure

Managers may see the app as surveillance; the category's top complaint elsewhere is staff simply not entering data. Counters: genuinely <3-minute submission, value for the manager (own records, protection against false accusation, price updates), owner-side nags — and the employer's authority. Validate daily compliance in week one of the pilot; it is the #1 risk.

Market volatility as product risk

Margins are politically exposed (2025 price-cap episodes squeezed retailers below breakeven for weeks). If stations struggle, subscriptions are early casualties. Price the app against leakage recovered, not against margin.

Competitor response

Epump could ship a hardware-free tier; Vendra could localize for Nigeria. The window is real but not permanent — speed to a working pilot with the client's own stations matters more than feature completeness.
